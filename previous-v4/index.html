Escaping convergence through scaffolding artistic plurality in AI

Generative AI pulls work towards its most statistically likely outcome. We are building process-oriented tools that let artists branch, map and steer through a model, and that make that pull visible.

About

01

Most generative tools are optimised for a fast, resolved output. That favours whatever the model has seen most.

The Antigravity Machine treats a model's latent space as somewhere to explore: a space artists can move through, fork and return to, with the pressures towards convergence shown instead of hidden.

Led by Dr Phoenix Perry at UAL's Creative Computing Institute, this twelve-month project began in September 2026 and brings together researchers at UAL, Simon Fraser University, York University and the University of Dundee. It is one of the projects funded through the AI Humanities Sandpit, part of Doing AI Differently.

What we are building

02

The software

Open-source software built to run locally on consumer hardware, with non-linear forking, persistent journey mapping, controls for output indeterminacy, and attractor diagnostics.

Expansion packs

Lightweight, artist-tuned add-ons that put the artist in control of which cultural traditions to draw on and which dominant patterns to resist.

IDEA framework

Interpretive Depth Evaluation for Artistic AI. It assesses how well an AI tool supports a creator's own goals rather than scoring the art.

Critical Response Guide

A companion guide for practitioners, based on Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process.

Installation and performance

A public installation on the Media Wall at Factory International in Manchester, and a live performance at Venue 13 in the 2027 Edinburgh Festival Fringe, both made with the software.

Energy dashboard

A live view of what each session costs in energy.
