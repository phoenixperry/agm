# Slow Levitation

*The algorithmic philosophy behind the Antigravity Machine background.
Written with the algorithmic-art skill (anthropics/skills) as the brief
for `agm-background.js`. The animation itself is the expression of it.*

The letters of the mark do not travel; they are carried. An invisible
body of air fills the page: a flow field that turns slowly through time,
and every letter rides it the way a feather rides a room's draughts.
Nothing is told where to go. Each letter has weight, an inertia of a
couple of seconds, so it leans into a current long after the current
has changed, and slides out of an eddy instead of snapping to it. A
faint lift runs through everything, different for each letter, so the
field as a whole rises: antigravity as a tendency, never as a command.
The algorithm must feel meticulously tuned: speeds measured in a few
pixels per second, inertia, lift and turbulence balanced against each
other until no single parameter announces itself.

The air is built from curl noise. A smooth, layered noise surface is
treated as a stream function, and the field is its rotated gradient, so
the flow is incompressible: it swirls, folds and shears, but it has no
sinks. There is nowhere in it for anything to converge. Letters may pass
close and part again; they never pile up at an attractor. Rotation comes
from the same source. Each letter samples the vorticity around it and
turns as the air turns, so a letter caught in an eddy slowly wheels, and
one in a straight current holds still. This is the product of deep
computational care: one field, read three ways (velocity, vorticity,
cohesion), so every movement on the page shares a single cause.

Coming apart is a property of place, not of time. A second, slower noise
field measures turbulence across the page. Where it runs high, a
letter's edges loosen: they peel from their corners, drift outward, bow
into gentle curves and tremble on their own noise. Where it runs low,
the edges find each other again and close into the letter. No timer
drives this. A letter unravels because of where the air has taken it,
and re-forms when the air lets it, so the rhythm of making and unmaking
is irregular, local and impossible to predict, the way a crafted
generative system should be: master-level restraint, never a loop you
can count.

Depth is quiet but real. Each letter is given a depth when it is born:
nearer letters are larger, a shade darker and carried a little faster;
farther ones are small, pale and slow. When the page scrolls, letters
move with it in proportion to their depth, so the field opens into
gentle parallax instead of lurching as one sheet. Scroll speed is felt
through a soft, low-passed measure, so a jerky mouse wheel still reads
as a breath. The cursor stirs the air rather than pushing the letters:
it adds a small, decaying swirl where it passes and raises the
turbulence there, so letters near it loosen of their own accord.

The one moment of convergence belongs to the hero. At the top of the
page the mark is whole, held in full ink. Scrolling releases its
letters one by one into the field, where the same air takes them and
their ink thins to hairline. Returning to the top calls them home on a
critically damped spring: they arc back, overshoot by nothing, turn
upright and close their edges as they arrive. That is the whole
argument of the project in miniature: the pull towards one resolved
form exists and can be shown, and the field around it is plural,
incompressible and free. Every number in this system should look as if
it was arrived at through countless iterations by someone at the very
top of the craft, because it was meant to be.
